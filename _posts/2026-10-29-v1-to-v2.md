## The short diary
The day started at about 00:10 UTC with the nightly shutdown recovery: an automation runbook plus a VM start. That closes the loose end from yesterday.

Morning was v1 polish. Versions 1.20 and 1.21 added a nine-skins page and an overlap scanner. Then v1.39 brought an allowance price tile with a detail panel. v1.48 restructured the report into an executive summary with a buy / hold / sell opinion, a detail analysis and an 8-graph appendix with an allocation-gap model sheet. And v1.49 made reports follow the browser language, Korean or English. For the mock data the opinion is: no purchase needed, and part of the surplus could be sold. The thresholds and safety-margin formula are assumptions, and I'm saying so plainly. Then I saved v1 with a record, a tag and a commit.

Then the side quest. I'd had a separate VM built with a Docker stack to study an open-source repo structure. It was interesting. It was also not the main line. I said "stop this", and everything got deleted: the resource group, the directory apps, the local keys.

Cleanup wasn't smooth. A delete lock on the DNS zone blocked removing one record, and the assistant did not lift the lock on its own initiative. Good. Giving a managed identity a directory permission needed an administrator and a long detour, first cloud shell errors, then a just-in-time role.

Afternoon: v2. It has its own address, its own container app and its own image, so v1 stays untouched. The design: no primary colors, a warm near-black first, then lightened into a warm graphite with champagne gold. A thin icon-rail menu that overlays, a Ctrl/Cmd+K command palette, a shrunken map with softened edges and a country strip, a staggered entrance animation, and two skins. Versions v2.1 to v2.9.

My feedback was quick. I loved the new map. The first look was "too dark", so it got lighter. Empty space at the bottom got three insight cards: year-end allowance outlook for Korea, plants closest to limit, and today's AI briefing.

At the end I created a public diary blog on GitHub Pages under my personal account. It was renamed twice. I asked for English, casual tone, first person, no project acronym, and VS Code as the platform.

## What got better
- Reports now lead with one line, buy, hold or sell, and the evidence follows.
- Empty space now carries information that serves the goal rather than decoration.
- v1 and v2 are independent, so experimenting with v2 can't break v1.
- The overlap scanner catches what eyes miss.

## Issues
- The side quest was a detour from the main line. I stopped it and deleted everything.
- A delete lock on the DNS zone blocked removing one record. The lock was not removed on the assistant's own initiative, and I handled it directly.
- Granting a managed identity a directory permission needed an administrator. It took a long detour through cloud shell errors, then a just-in-time role.
- v2 first looked too dark, so I lightened the base.
- The buy / hold / sell opinion depends on assumed thresholds and an assumed safety-margin formula, so it's mock-data advice only.

## What I learned
> One sentence that protects the main line beats a well-built side quest. And a lock, annoying as it is, gets a question before it gets removed.

- Look at the screen in a loop.
- Scanners catch what eyes miss.
- Fill empty space with something useful.
